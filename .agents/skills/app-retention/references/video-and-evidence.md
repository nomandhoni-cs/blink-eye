# Video, claims, and creator follow-ups

This pack adapts App Breakdown’s [*How to design an app SO addictive people cant stop using it*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NHIq3HnokOI) (November 13, 2026; 15:16). The walkthrough below paraphrases the public captions; use the original video’s [Show transcript option](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15930243?hl=en) for verbatim captions. The creator’s framing and examples are not scientific findings by themselves. Source IDs resolve to the [source library](source-library.md) and the [indexed references](../assets/research-source-index.csv).

## Chapter map

App Breakdown splits the video into getting people in (steps 1–3) and keeping them (steps 4–6). The transcript identifies these topics and examples; it does **not** establish that every named app deliberately uses an identical six-step system or that the six steps work as a package. The video is a design teardown, not a controlled study. [Source video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NHIq3HnokOI)

## Claims worth correcting before making a product decision

| Claim in the video | What the source supports | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| “93 apps” installed; “about half” opened monthly | App Annie’s **2019** estimate was 93 installed and 41 used monthly (about 44%), as reported in 2020. The original page did not load when checked; this is historical industry data, not a current universal average. [TechCrunch / App Annie](https://techcrunch.com/2020/07/27/top-mobile-apps-see-declines-in-consumer-engagement-amid-increased-competition/) | Measure your users’ real need, not a historical industry average. |
| About half use a phone one-handed | Hoober (2013) observed 1,333 public phone uses; **49%** of the **780 people observed actively touching** a device used a one-handed grip. The method, devices, and setting limit generalization. [Original field notes](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2013/02/how-do-users-really-hold-mobile-devices.php) | Test reach in both hands on current devices. |
| Cal AI got 15M+ downloads because its action was simpler | A TechCrunch event profile reports **15M+ downloads in under two years**. That is not a retention number, and the comparison does not isolate ease of use as the cause. Yazio also documents photo-based estimates with review/edit options, so the teardown is an example of a particular path/version, not a permanent feature difference. [TechCrunch event profile](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/24/techcrunch-disrupt-2026-cal-ais-zach-yadegari-on-how-to-create-viral-growth-and-capitalize-on-it/) [Yazio photo logging](https://help.yazio.com/hc/en-us/articles/208846625-How-can-I-record-food-from-restaurants-or-canteens) | Compare task time **and** error rate; make estimated results editable. |
| Duolingo retention rose 3.3% | Duolingo’s 2020 A/B test reported **+3.3% relative Day-14 retention**, **+1% relative overall daily active learners**, and **+10.5% relative** in the share of daily learners on a streak within 20 days. Fewer learners reached their full daily goal. The post does not make the result a learning-outcome estimate or a benchmark. [Duolingo’s experiment](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak/) | Separate a small meaningful win from the deeper outcome. At a hypothetical 20% baseline, +3.3% relative is **20.66%**, or **+0.66 percentage points**, not 23.3%. |
| “23% vs. 86%” of people actually donate organs | Johnson & Goldstein (2003) reported **42% opt-in, 82% opt-out, 79% active choice** in a hypothetical online experiment (161 people); opt-out and active choice did not differ significantly. Their historical country chart was **4.25–27.5% vs. 85.9–99.98% effective consent**, not actual donations or two overall population rates. Actual donations were a separate, observational analysis; review evidence notes the role of infrastructure and other factors. [Johnson & Goldstein full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8996952_Do_Defaults_Save_Lives) [NIHR review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK56888/) | Defaults affect choices. Measure the downstream outcome separately. |
| TikTok hooks people at *exactly* 260 videos | NPR and Kentucky Public Radio reported **260 videos** from internal material cited in 2024 litigation. The complaint extrapolated “under 35 minutes” from clips as short as eight seconds (260 × 8 seconds ≈ 34 minutes 40 seconds), not from a measured clinical threshold. The underlying internal study is not publicly available in this source set. TikTok disputed the reporting’s characterization of the material. [NPR reporting](https://www.npr.org/2024/10/11/g-s1-27676/tiktok-redacted-documents-in-teen-safety-lawsuit-revealed) [Kentucky Public Radio](https://www.lpm.org/news/2024-10-11/listen-kentuckys-tiktok-lawsuit-revealed-executives-knew-about-apps-effect-on-teens) | Never design toward a count of views meant to create compulsion. |
| One Skinner experiment explains both the slot machine and Tinder | The 1957 reinforcement-schedule monograph and the partial-reinforcement literature are the relevant match for the video’s account. The exact two-group protocol as narrated is not identified. The frequently cited 1948 “Superstition” study was **different**: food arrived on a timer independent of behavior. Tinder co-founder Jonathan Badeen’s inspiration was reported from a documentary interview; it is not evidence of a causal effect on app users. [Foundation monograph](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/05/Schedules_of_Reinforcement_PDF.pdf) [Skinner 1948 reprint](https://www.bfskinner.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Superstition-in-the-pigeon.pdf) [Recode interview at Vox](https://www.vox.com/2018/9/19/17877004/nancy-jo-sales-swiped-hbo-documentary-tinder-dating-app-addictive-pigeon-kara-swisher-decode-podcast) | Borrow variety or good feedback, not randomized jackpots. |
| “100,000 lives” wasted every day | Raskin said “on the order of a hundred thousand human **lifetimes** per day” in a TED podcast. It is his rough aggregate time estimate, not lives literally lost or a peer-reviewed measurement. [Raskin interview transcript](https://www.ted.com/podcasts/rethinking-with-adam-grant/aza-raskin-on-why-technology-and-democracy-are-in-an-imagination-crisis-transcript) | Use actual task completion and user-reported control in your product. |
| Netflix saves $1B/year because users would lose their history | Netflix’s authors estimated **more than US$1B/year** for **personalization and recommendations combined** in their 2015 ACM paper, discussing churn and subscriber replacement. This historical company estimate does not isolate dependence on history and is not an independently audited result or a forecast. [Netflix paper, §3](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/2843948) | Test whether returning users find value faster; avoid making cancellation or data export harder. |

## Creator follow-ups: Yazio and Finch

These are observations of specific app versions in the same creator’s series, not controlled studies.

| Source | What the walkthrough shows | What to take to your backlog |
|---|---|---|
| V01 · **Yazio #60**: [First opening](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TZwlxUiiar0&t=27s), [onboarding feedback](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TZwlxUiiar0&t=154s), [food logging](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TZwlxUiiar0&t=827s), [photo feature](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TZwlxUiiar0&t=1151s) | A notification request precedes value; the long questionnaire uses mascot feedback and a hold-to-commit gesture. The meal `+` leads to another step; the creator later finds a Pro AI camera and praises the flexible recipe filters. The companion page documents a discount wheel after the paywall. | Put logging and a repeat-entry shortcut before optional questions. If setup is truly necessary, show the result of each meaningful step and provide `Skip` where possible; a hold gesture needs an accessible alternative. Show pricing plainly. For a browsing task keep useful filters, even if a single CTA is best for logging. |
| V02 · **Finch #55**: [Hatch a pet](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xjhy91FlemQ&t=63s), [streak discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xjhy91FlemQ&t=393s), [widget](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xjhy91FlemQ&t=473s), [goal loop](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xjhy91FlemQ&t=603s), [notification after an adventure starts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xjhy91FlemQ&t=822s) | Small goals let people `Complete`, `Skip` or `Snooze`; completing a goal visibly energizes the pet and can unlock an adventure. The reminder explains what event it will announce. The walkthrough notes that a finished goal list immediately suggests adding more. | Reward the real action and allow pausing without penalty. Make “All done for today” a valid outcome. Let an optional widget resume a chosen action, not display an invented emergency. Don’t credit streak days the person hasn’t completed. |

**Claims not to carry forward.** A Yazio onboarding screen saying “Only 57% get this far” supplies no cohort, time window, or independent measurement. Finch’s widget pitch says users with it are “4× more likely” to stick to self-care habits; no underlying report or assignment method was found. People who choose widgets may already be more motivated. If a widget might help your app, first test discovery and task completion; to estimate incremental impact, randomize an optional widget invitation and compare **assigned groups**, not widget installers versus everyone else.
